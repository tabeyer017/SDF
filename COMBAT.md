# Combat and damage control

The damage model and performance values below are a first playable balance pass. SDF and pirate weapon quantities and mounting arrangements use the confirmed class fits. Civilian fits remain provisional (one mount per listed weapon without a confirmed fit). Each missile type has one provisional firing channel per ship; missile inventory is not a launcher count.

The pirate corvette has one forward 120 mm autocannon turret and 30 ventral-launched GP missiles. The pirate frigate has two single-gun 120 mm turrets (one forward, one aft atop the shuttle hangar) and 40 ventral-launched GP missiles. Both have two 30 mm CIWS mounts (one port, one starboard), chaff, flares, a radar jammer, one bay with four assault shuttles, 40 raiders, and two engines. Chaff/flare deployment and shuttle operations remain future simulation work; mounting locations are catalog information rather than simulated firing arcs.

Earlier saves with provisional pirate fits upgrade on load. Existing hull/system damage, expended ammunition, firing-cycle timers, repair jobs, surrender state, and player progress are retained. Added mounts inherit their former weapon group's damage and firing-cycle time; inventory capacity increases retain the number of rounds already expended. Corvette MP/75 mm mounts become GP/120 mm mounts, while projectiles already in flight keep their original weapon, damage and guidance.

## Confirmed targeting and projectile rules

- Guns use ship radar, laser range finding, and laser targeting.
- Missiles guide with onboard radar and a short-range IR fallback against warm, powered targets within 20 km. IR becomes more useful at closer range; cold or distant targets provide no IR fallback. Previously launched missiles do not depend on the firing ship's radar remaining active.
- Every 30 mm CIWS mount has an independent targeting computer, tracking radar, and tracking IR. Its tracking continues if the ship's main radar or fire-control sensors are disabled, provided that mount and main power still work.
- Choose missile targets directly with **Aim at system**. There is no separate missile-role preset.
- MP missiles make precision subsystem strikes: engines, weapon mounts, radar / fire control, radar jammer, reactor, power, EPD, bridge, communications, or life support. They cannot select a whole-ship hull strike or intercept incoming missiles. Subsystem hits still inflict collateral hull damage under the shared damage model.
- GP missiles can attack hulls, any subsystem, or incoming missiles. AA use against shuttles and fighters remains part of the setting; those entities are not yet spawned.
- AS missiles carry the heavy anti-ship warhead and can strike the hull or any ship subsystem. Internal systems remain protected until the hull reaches zero; AS missiles cannot intercept incoming missiles. Their current damage remains 160, compared with GP 90 and MP 60.
- Radar aim selects anti-radiation guidance. A jammer aim selects anti-jammer guidance while its emitter is active, or radar / IR precision guidance against silent hardware. GP, MP and autocannons can all target the jammer. Guidance and the target system are fixed when launched; switching off an emitter breaks an existing emission-homing lock.
- All projectiles inherit the firing ship's velocity. Ballistic rounds retain their resulting velocity in the sector frame. Guided missiles add their configured flight speed in a fixed launch frame and continually steer toward a predicted interception point; later changes to the firing ship's course do not drag missiles around. Damage does not fall with distance. A missile launched at a target matching the ship's 7,200 KPH velocity therefore adds 3,600 KPH of closing speed, rather than dropping to 3,600 KPH absolute speed and falling behind both ships.
- Setting note only: weapons that miss eventually self-destruct, including after an appropriate travel distance. Self-destruction distance, timers, debris, and stray missed projectiles are not simulated. A miss is resolved and discarded.

## Initial weapon performance

Rates are per barrel or missile firing channel. Range is the maximum launch/fire-control range, not damage falloff. Travel is simulated in kilometres and simulated seconds. Projectile speeds below are added to the firing ship's launch velocity. These speed values are game assumptions, not real weapon specifications.

Player missile launchers fire **1, 5, 10, or 15 missiles** per launch, selected with **Missile volley size** in Fire Control or the quick-fire popup. The default is **1 missile at a time**, shared by manual fire and Tactical Disable / Destroy orders; the captain can choose a larger volley. Changes apply to the next launch, including active orders, and persist in saves and across encounters. Each missile has its own hit assessment, warhead, and CIWS interception track. A low inventory launches the remaining missiles. The launch cycle scales with the number fired to preserve the listed average rounds/minute; larger volleys do not refill ammunition or increase the sustained launch rate.

**Cannon burst size** sits beside **Missile volley size** in both panels; each dropdown shows numbers only. Choose **1, 5, 10, or 20 total autocannon shells per mount**, default **5**. Each mount fires one shell at a time, consuming one round and making an independent hit check at each firing cycle. Full-condition 120 mm mounts fire every 3 simulated seconds with one barrel, or every 1.5 seconds with two barrels alternating. Each successful shot has its own trajectory and radar dot. Manual Fire stops after one burst; Start Firing and Tactical Disable / Destroy repeat until stopped or overridden, adding a **2-second pause** after the final shell's normal firing cycle. A five-shell single-barrel burst fires at 0, 3, 6, 9 and 12 seconds, with the next automatic burst starting at 17 seconds. Changing the size applies to the next burst. Preferences, unfinished bursts and cooldowns persist in saves; older saves default to 5. Low stocks fire only the remaining ammunition. Rail guns and CIWS retain their own firing cycles.

**Missiles and autocannons do not reload during combat.** They fire from their remaining ready inventory at the listed firing cadence, without an additional magazine or launcher reload delay. A depleted weapon stays empty until replenished by a suitable station or an arrived resupply/repair tender; waiting, onboard repairs, and entering another sector do not restore rounds. A multi-barrel turret can use its final round even if it cannot fire every barrel together.

Rail-gun turrets discharge their barrels together and consume ammunition per barrel; autocannon turrets fire individual shells in sequence. The **30 mm CIWS is belt-fed from a drum and does not pause to reload between bursts or targets**. Its provisional firing rate is 3,000 rounds/minute (50 rounds/second) per barrel. Manual ship attacks use ten-round firing slices, lasting 0.2 seconds at full condition; continuous fire feeds the next slice at the weapon's firing cadence. The Tactical console and armament catalog identify its belt-fed drum, and the status calls this firing/cycling rather than reloading.

Automatic CIWS defaults on and engages incoming missiles within 20 km. Each mount independently reserves one missile and up to 75 rounds, firing them over 1.5 simulated seconds at full condition before an interception assessment. A full engagement has an 85% interception chance at full condition; a short final burst uses the remaining drum ammunition with proportionally lower chance. The next available threat can be engaged immediately after that firing slice, without a separate reload delay. Damage reduces firing rate and interception chance. Separate mounts choose separate threats. Close launches, exhausted ammunition, damaged mounts, failed attempts, and large salvos can allow missiles through. Active cycles and ammunition persist in saves; older 10–15 second engagement delays shorten to the current firing duration without restoring spent rounds. CIWS does not intercept cannon shells or rail-gun rounds. With a GP mount, choose **Incoming missile · AA** under **Aim at system**, then choose the incoming missile from the AA target selector.

Hit probability depends on range, fire-control condition, and radar jamming; impact damage depends on armor and penetration, not distance. Jammers now reduce acquisition / fire-control track quality. Interference increases with target distance, jammer condition and power, and weakens with better receiver condition or closer approach. At high interference, the ship has an emitter bearing but no precision lock: normal weapons wait without spending ammunition, while GP / MP can still home on the active jammer. Tracking-dependent maneuvers cancel and coast when precision tracking is lost. IFF identification is retained separately. Radar markers gain a dashed gold emission ring, and the radar, hover readout and target panel show jammer emissions and track quality.

An active radar jammer can also defeat an incoming AS, MP, or GP missile completely, causing zero hull or subsystem damage. Each missile gets one guidance contest when it first encounters active jamming within the 40 km terminal envelope, including a jammer switched on after launch. The provisional full-condition disruption chance is 45%, scaled with jammer condition and power. Within 20 km, IR fallback against a warm powered target reduces that chance, increasingly at closer range; it is not immunity. A missile that loses guidance farther out cannot recover using IR it never reaches. Each missile in a volley is assessed separately, and a completed contest is saved rather than rerolled per frame or on reload. Anti-jammer missiles home on the emitter and bypass this disruption. Main radar can be silenced; passive coverage has a provisional 60 km baseline, compared with 200 km active radar. Sensor damage reduces coverage.

**Radar burn-through** in Fire Control and the quick-fire popup gives **15 simulated seconds** of high-power tracking through jamming, followed by a **180-second minimum interval between uses**. It requires operational active radar and power, cannot extend sensor coverage or repair damaged sensors, and cannot be used while docked / tender-connected or during EPD. It restores the firing ship's tracking, rather than disabling the target's jammer or protecting a missile's separate onboard seeker. A cooling status prevents continuous use. Pulse and cooldown times persist in saves, and EPD cancels an active pulse without refunding its cooldown.

Jammers default to **AUTO: combat only** on every ship, including pirates. Only ships in a live engagement, exchanging shots within the local 200 km combat envelope, or actively retreating emit; routine traffic and uninvolved ships stay silent. The player button cycles **AUTO → ON → SILENT → AUTO**, preserving deliberate overrides in saves. Emitting reveals the ship to passive receivers out to their condition-limited 200 km emitter-detection range, even outside normal passive coverage, without automatically revealing IFF or making it hostile. Damaged/offline jammers, power loss and surrender stop emissions. Radar jammers are confirmed on both pirate classes; jammer hardware on other classes remains provisional.

In Tactical, check one or more weapon mounts. Expand **Targeting** for a selected mount to choose its own locked contact and aim system. The default **Active radar target** follows the current radar selection for new commands; add multiple locks with Ctrl + click to assign different contacts to different weapons. MP and AS targeting restrictions still apply per mount.

Right-clicking an enemy, neutral, or unknown radar contact opens compact fire control: mount checkboxes, an aim dropdown per mount, and individual **Fire** and **Start Firing** buttons for each mount. Each individual button checks that mount and issues an order only for it; the group buttons still operate on all checked mounts. These controls edit the same mount selections and aim values as the right-hand column, which also provides individual mount buttons. The clicked contact becomes the selected aim target for new commands without releasing other locks or changing active orders. **×**, an outside click, or **Escape** dismisses the popup. It also closes when leaving Tactical, losing the contact's live track, loading/resetting a game, or resizing the viewport.

Right-clicking a green friendly SDF ship opens commands without a priority prompt or changes to player locks. **Attack my targets** transmits live, identified hostile locks in priority order. **I need assistance in this battle** uses confirmed hostile threats, without requiring a lock. The ship refuses unsafe or impossible requests: critical damage or emergencies, hostile relations, disabled bridge/power/fire control, or no compatible weapons, ammunition, or launch range. A working live communications link is required. Accepted orders start local time and use the ally's own ammunition, cooldowns, weapon ranges, damage model, and CIWS; MP missiles target engines, and other offensive weapons target hulls. Enemies can retaliate against supporting allies. Support does not alter ship courses or the player's weapon assignments. **Cease fire / stand down** stops that ally's support order; existing shots continue. **Report your status** reports hull condition and support activity. Requests and replies appear in Comms; support assignments persist in saves and clear with EPD departure and encounter replacement. Surrender/cease-fire removes that target from support orders, and critical damage or emergencies stop an ally's support.

Internal aim points (reactor, power distribution, EPD, bridge, communications, and life support) are greyed out and unselectable while the target has any hull points. Engines, weapon mounts, radar, and jammer are exposed equipment and can be targeted before the hull is breached. At exactly zero hull, surviving internal systems become selectable. Zero-point entries are red, marked **0 · DESTROYED**, and cannot be selected. An engine or weapon category reaches zero only when every component in that category is destroyed.

When the chosen aim point becomes unavailable, the dropdown advances to the next surviving, exposed entry in list order, wrapping as needed. Active continuous-fire orders advance by the same rule before the next launch. This never changes the target ship or redirects a shot already in flight. AS missiles follow the same rule and continue against surviving systems after hull destruction; automatic aim never switches a ship strike into an incoming-missile engagement.

**Fire** starts one finite autocannon burst, or fires each other ready selected mount once (a ten-round firing slice for CIWS). Unavailable mounts are skipped with an individual reason. **Start firing** starts or updates an independent order for each valid selected mount, including mounts currently in a firing cycle. Each mount repeats at its own rate, consumes its own weapon inventory, and retains the target and aim captured when ordered; changing the radar selection does not redirect an existing order. Unchecking a mount in either control panel cancels its unfinished burst and continuous-fire order; checking it again only selects it until a firing command is issued. A failed or destroyed mount stops only its own order. **Cease all fire**, available in the main column and popup, cancels every unfinished player burst and firing order and unchecks all mounts in both panels. **Clear selection** does the same. Firing starts the shared local simulation.

**Disable ships / Destroy ships** follow locked-target priority. Against a live ship with surviving CIWS, Tactical directs autocannons and any rail guns at **CIWS mounts**, destroying each in turn, and holds missiles until the last CIWS mount is destroyed. Next, guns and **MP missiles** target an active **radar jammer**; AS missiles wait until the jammer is destroyed or stops emitting. Main radar loss does not count as CIWS or jammer suppression. Guns must be in range and operational for CIWS suppression; MP missiles can engage the jammer beyond autocannon range. After suppression, Disable targets engines and weapons, while Destroy attacks the hull and surviving exposed systems. Our own CIWS remains reserved for missile defense. Manual weapon orders override Tactical per mount, so the captain can deliberately launch a volley before suppression completes. **CIWS mounts** is also available as a manual subsystem aim point. Both pirate classes use the same defensive-system priorities, using **autocannons** against CIWS, then **autocannons and GP missiles** against the jammer, before resuming other ship attacks. Pirates launch one missile at a time.

Armed ships attacked by the player return fire with available weapons. Randomized arrival encounters can also include pirates already prepared to attack once local time runs. Cease fire does not stop hostile retaliation or weapons already in flight. Pause local time to pause the whole encounter. Ships remain local encounter traffic; autonomous pursuit and persistent interplanetary NPC travel are not implemented.

## Enemy surrender and disengagement

Engaged opponents now decide when to disengage or surrender without waiting for a demand. Pirates consider retreat around **60% hull**, SDF crews around **45%**, and other hostile ships around **55%**, with a stable ±5 percentage-point variation per track. Hull damage alone triggers retreat only when the odds are unfavorable. Most offensive mounts disabled / empty, all offensive ammunition at 10% or less, or loss of both CIWS and jammer under sustained missile pressure also encourage retreat. Quiet damaged traffic does not panic, and destroying the jammer alone does not force surrender. Individual decisions do not reroll every frame or on reload.

Retreating ships stop offensive fire, turn their velocity away from the nearest attacker and accelerate at their surviving engine performance toward the existing 7,200 KPH sector limit. CIWS remains available and automatic jamming remains active during the escape. Existing momentum is retained; even a ship with destroyed engines can try to coast away if it is already opening the range fast enough. The captain can pursue a retreating contact. There is no NPC EPD departure in this implementation.

A cornered ship offers surrender when hull reaches **40% or below**, its offensive weapons are exhausted / disabled, or it has a severe fire / life-support emergency with no more than 300 seconds of reserve air. Cornered means propulsion cannot provide an escape (and momentum cannot carry it away), or a retreat has failed to gain meaningful separation for at least 20 simulated seconds. A breached, disabled hull also ends resistance. Surrender secures weapons, radar, jammer and CIWS, clears player and allied firing assignments against that ship, and preserves unrelated engagements and all projectiles already in flight. Manual surrender demands retain their existing 40%-hull / all-weapons-destroyed acceptance rule.

Tactical shows **DISENGAGING** or **SURRENDERED**, and Comms receives one retreat report or surrender offer per transition. An offline enemy communications array produces a sensor-watch report instead of a fabricated voice transmission. A retreating ship outside actual sensor / emitter-detection coverage generates **Contact lost**, retains its last-known sensor record, and can be reacquired; the game does not claim a confirmed escape or delete the ship. Decisions, retreat velocity, blocked-escape time and reported-notice state persist in JSON saves. EPD travel to another sector replaces the old encounter normally.

The frigate baseline is bridge 100, reactor 200, power distribution 150, EPD 160, sensors 80, communications 60, life support 140, radar jammer 60, and each engine 120 points. Other classes scale those points by hull/1,000, with a 20-point minimum. Every main engine has its own damage state; engine counts follow the existing catalog.

Every mount has its own points: autocannon turret 80, CIWS mount 50, rail-gun turret 160, missile firing installation 100. A disabled twin-gun turret disables both barrels. Hull points and equipment points are separate indicators. A hit on an available aim point reduces hull integrity by its post-armor damage and the selected equipment by 30% of that damage. Internal systems cannot be damaged through an intact hull. After the hull is breached, equipment damage continues with hull integrity held at zero. Aim categories engines/weapons select the first functioning component in that category. Damage location is abstracted by aim system; physical section geometry and directional armor are not simulated.

Condition bands are above 70% operational, 40–70% degraded (70% performance), below 40% critical (35% performance), and zero offline. Engine condition, reactor and power distribution affect thrust; existing velocity is preserved when thrust is lost. Turret damage slows firing cadence. Sensor damage reduces acquisition range and turret accuracy. Main power loss disables weapons. An offline EPD prevents strategic engagement. The bridge is tracked but does not yet enforce a separate command-delay penalty; communications damage does not yet block station-service requests. A reactor reaching zero shuts down rather than automatically exploding.

Hull below 50% causes pressure loss. Critical reactor/power hits can cause a fire. Fire consumes hull integrity; pressure loss, offline life support, or power loss consume a 1,800-second emergency air reserve. Exhausted air reduces hull to zero. At zero hull the ship is disabled, with no operational power, thrust, sensors, or weapons, but its surviving systems remain targetable. A player ship at zero hull cannot continue operating. Individual crew casualties and compartment propagation are not implemented.

## Repair and replenishment

Engineering owns the damage-control screen, repair queue, and docking/service controls. Operations reports spare-parts inventory and teams. Security and Environment can isolate fires and pressure loss. Command and Operations link to Engineering.

Engineering defaults to **Automatic repairs: ON**, including saves created before this setting existed. Repairable damage automatically joins the onboard queue; hull, life support, reactor, power, and engines take priority when new automatic jobs are assigned. Manual orders go ahead of automatic jobs. **Automatic repairs: OFF** stops automatic jobs and preserves manual orders and station service. Canceling an onboard job holds that system out of automatic scheduling; **Repair manually · held** clears its hold and queues a manual order. Re-enabling automatic repairs clears individual holds. The mode, holds, automatic jobs, and partial repair labor persist in JSON saves and browser autosaves. Automatic repairs respect pause, spare-parts limits, field ceilings, and destroyed-system restrictions; they never request docking or station service on your behalf.

Each repair team restores 10 points per simulated minute, or 5 points/minute for 30 seconds after a hit. The queue assigns one job to each available team. Each completed repair block of up to 5 points consumes one whole spare-parts unit; labor toward the next block is retained in saves. Canceling discards unfinished labor but never refunds consumed supplies. No points can be restored onboard once parts run out.

Field repairs stop at 60% hull integrity and 75% equipment integrity. A system at zero requires station replacement. Emergency isolation consumes no spare parts and restores no damage points. Repair jobs and emergencies continue during EPD transit as shipboard time advances; players should contain emergencies before departure.

Dock within 5 km of the current station's arrival point at 10 KPH or less, with no incoming missiles or live engaged opponents within 200 km. Docking cancels movement orders and holds the ship at rest. Undock before local maneuvering or EPD. Station service must be explicitly requested; station arrival alone does not replenish the ship.

The six lunar stations, Heliopause station, and Mars colony provide full service: 50 points/minute/team, replacement of destroyed systems, and repair to 100%, followed by 60 seconds for full spare-parts/ammunition replenishment. Station resources pay for these repairs; the player's empty inventory does not prevent service. Service also contains emergencies. Mining stations provide field-level repairs, replenish spares up to at least half capacity, and restock gun ammunition; they do not replace destroyed systems or stock missiles. There is no credit economy or station-stock depletion yet.

If the ship cannot reach a station, choose **Request resupply / repair ship** in Engineering or send that request to Earth HQ or a station through Comms. A repair tender approaches the current ship position at a provisional 1 km/s, starting about three simulated minutes away. The green **TENDER** marker appears on Tactical Radar when within the selected coverage. It waits until the ship holds below 10 KPH within 5 km and has no incoming fire or live nearby engaged opponents. Local time must run for the rendezvous and service to advance.

The connected tender repairs systems to full condition, including destroyed engines, at full station-service rates, then transfers ammunition and spare parts after repairs and a 60-second transfer period. No ammunition is granted merely by sending the request or reaching the rendezvous. Offensive firing, local maneuvers, and EPD are unavailable while connected; cancel the tender service in Engineering to depart. Incoming threats or loss of the rendezvous suspend service without replenishment. EPD departure cancels a pending dispatch. Tender travel, service progress, and remaining ammunition persist in saves. The logistics tender is a service vessel rather than another selectable combat target.

## Persistence

Version 2 JSON saves and browser autosaves include all player and encounter damage, ammunition, per-mount cooldowns, radar/jammer/CIWS settings, queued repairs and partial labor, emergencies, pending successful shots with their launch guidance, independent per-mount firing assignments, engagement orders, and station-service progress. Older single firing orders migrate to the list of mount orders. Active firing assignments reopen checked with their saved target and aim; unissued checkbox and targeting edits are session controls. Legacy missile-role fields and in-flight MP AA shots remain readable, but old presets do not control new launches. Loading pauses local and strategic time; it does not reroll shots already in flight. Version 1 saves migrate automatically with undamaged ships and full stores.

Missile saves now include their fixed launch velocity. Older in-flight missiles that lack this field adopt the firing vessel's current velocity once when the patrol is loaded/refreshed, then retain that frame independently. Their original launch velocity cannot be reconstructed if the vessel has already changed course since launch. This migration corrects the pending missile heading without advancing time, moving ships, replenishing ammunition or rerolling guidance assessments. A destroyed jammer cannot jam normal radar / IR missiles; emission-homing shots still follow their existing emitter-lock rules.

New sector arrivals generate destination-dependent traffic and events, replacing local contacts and clearing encounter fire orders/projectiles while retaining the player's damage, ammunition, spares, presets and onboard repair jobs. Distress scenarios initialize damaged civilian engines and hulls; pirate scenarios initialize hostile intent. Arrival is paused. Encounter identities and event selection persist with the evolved contact positions and combat damage, so loading does not reroll or reapply an event. Older training saves remain valid. EPD departure discards weapons left behind in the previous encounter; it does not repair or resupply the player. Restart patrol creates a fresh player ship and the initial Earth training encounter.
